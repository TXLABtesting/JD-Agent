01

Oracle Fusion Agentic AI in Plain Business Language

Most enterprise software is reactive: it waits for a person to navigate a screen, fill a form, and click submit. Generative AI added a helpful layer on top — it can draft an email, summarize a policy, or answer a question. Agentic AI goes one step further: it completes the work. Given a goal stated in plain language, an AI agent reasons about the steps required, calls the right systems and data, performs the transaction, and reports back — escalating to a human only where judgment or approval is required.

In practical terms, an Oracle Fusion AI agent combines four ingredients: a goal (what the user wants), reasoning (a plan to achieve it), tools (secure access to Fusion data and transactions), and guardrails (the policies, approvals, and role-based permissions that keep it safe). Because the agents are built inside Oracle Fusion rather than bolted on, they operate on live, governed enterprise data with the same security model the application already enforces — no data copies, no separate integration to maintain.

Key takeaway
Generative AI assists the user. Agentic AI acts for the user — within governed boundaries, on enterprise data, inside the application itself.
02

Multi-Agent Collaboration & the Supervisory (Orchestrator) Agent

No single agent tries to do everything. Instead, Oracle Fusion uses a team of specialized agents — each expert in one domain — coordinated by a Supervisory (Orchestrator) Agent. The orchestrator is the conductor: it receives the user's request, determines intent, breaks the request into tasks, routes each task to the right specialized agent, sequences their work and dependencies, and composes a single, coherent response. The user experiences one assistant; behind it, many agents collaborate.

Orchestrator responsibility What it does for the business
Intent recognitionUnderstands what the user is actually trying to accomplish from plain language.
Task decompositionSplits a complex request into discrete steps that individual agents can own.
Routing & delegationSends each step to the specialized agent best equipped to handle it.
Sequencing & dependenciesManages order of operations so outputs of one agent feed the next correctly.
Synthesis & responseCombines results into one clear answer or completed action for the user.
Guardrail enforcementApplies role-based security, approval rules, and policy at every hand-off.

Manager Workspace — The Conversational Front Door for People Leaders

The Manager Workspace is where a people leader meets the agent network. Instead of navigating menus, a manager simply asks — "How much leave does Sara have left?", "Approve Ahmed's absence", "Start a job-grade change for my team" — and the orchestrator routes the request to the right agent and completes it. Critically, the conversation is context-aware: the workspace knows who the manager is, who reports to them, who is in focus, and what was discussed earlier in the conversation, while still respecting role-based access controls.

Capability What it does Example request
Employee chatA conversational channel to ask about any team member or HR process."Show me Ahmed's profile and pending items."
Leave managementReview, approve, or decline leave requests directly in the conversation."Approve Sara's leave for next week."
Absence trackingSee who is out, upcoming absences, and coverage gaps across the team."Who is on leave this month?"
Leave balancesRetrieve accurate, real-time entitlement and remaining balances on demand."How many annual-leave days does Sara have left?"
HR changesInitiate transactions — transfers, grade or reporting changes — with approvals."Start a promotion for Ahmed to grade 2.3."
Context-aware conversationMaintains memory of who and what is in focus, so follow-ups need no repetition."…and what about her remaining sick leave?"

End-to-End Recruitment Agent Workflow

Recruitment is the clearest showcase of agentic value: a long, repetitive, multi-system process compressed into a guided flow where the agent does the heavy lifting and the recruiter makes the decisions. The workflow runs in seven stages, each with a human checkpoint before consequential actions.

Human in the loop
The agent compresses days of manual effort into minutes, but the recruiter approves the shortlist, confirms interviews, and signs the offer. Speed comes from the agent; the hiring decision stays with the human.

Integration with Oracle Calendar & the Manager Dashboard

Agentic AI is only useful if it plugs into the tools leaders already use. Two integrations anchor the experience. Oracle Calendar gives agents the ability to read availability, place tentative holds, resolve scheduling conflicts, and book or reschedule meetings — the backbone of interview scheduling and workspace planning. Responsible AI — Governance, Instructions & Bias Reduction

Oracle's approach to Responsible AI is built into how agents are designed and operated, not added afterward. Four pillars matter most to executive stakeholders.

Prompts & instructions
Agents operate within explicit, curated system instructions that define scope, tone, and the boundaries of what they may do — and are grounded in your enterprise data rather than open speculation.
Governance & oversight
Human approval gates, role-based permissions, audit trails, and transparency over what an agent did and why keep accountability with the organization.
Bias reduction
Curated data, fairness testing, explainable outputs, and guardrails reduce the risk of biased recommendations — especially in sensitive areas like pay and hiring.
Transparency & explainability
Outputs come with the reasoning and source context behind them — fit scores, recommendations, and summaries can be inspected and challenged by a person.
09

Enterprise Security Model

The single most common executive question is: where does our data go? The answer is that it stays inside Oracle. Agents run on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure within the customer's own isolated tenancy, operate on data that never leaves Oracle Fusion, and inherit the same role-based access controls and encryption the applications already enforce. Customer data is not used to train the underlying foundation models.
